# OPT 374 Human Visual Perception — Slide and Content Audit

## Purpose

This audit converts the supplied lecturer slides into an original, slide-aligned assessment package for the **Human Visual Perception** module in the Optometry Study Hub. It distinguishes:

1. **Course alignment** — what the lecturer expects students to know.
2. **Factual verification** — whether a claim is supported by established visual-neuroscience sources.
3. **Assessment suitability** — whether a claim can support an unambiguous, useful question.
4. **Implementation suitability** — whether the idea can be represented safely in the app’s nine-format assessment schema.

The supplied slides remain the primary source for course scope and terminology. External references are used to correct obvious errors, qualify oversimplifications, and avoid converting uncertain statements into misleading answer keys.

## Scope covered

The package covers the uploaded teaching block:

- sensation versus perception;
- bottom-up and top-down processing;
- figure-ground organization and selected visual illusions;
- broad organization and lateralization of the visual system;
- retinal layers and cell classes;
- rods, cones and ipRGCs;
- foveal and peripheral specialization;
- retinal ganglion-cell pathways;
- convergence and summation;
- centre-surround receptive fields and lateral inhibition;
- scotopic, mesopic and photopic vision;
- light and dark adaptation;
- LGN laminae and eye-specific inputs;
- magnocellular, parvocellular and koniocellular streams;
- V1 anatomy, layer inputs, retinotopy and receptive-field selectivity;
- ocular dominance, development and amblyopia;
- disparity tuning;
- extrastriate hierarchy;
- dorsal and ventral visual streams;
- akinetopsia, prosopagnosia, visual agnosia, cortical blindness and hemispatial neglect;
- image-forming and non-image-forming visual pathways.

## Scope not yet complete

The course outline lists later blocks that are not fully represented by the uploaded files:

- depth-perception mechanisms beyond the introductory examples;
- stereopsis and stereoacuity testing;
- colour-perception theory, anomalies and colour-vision testing;
- a full motion-perception unit;
- entoptic phenomena;
- a dedicated visual-illusions unit.

The current bank must therefore be described as a **foundations and visual-system-components bank**, not the complete OPT 374 course bank. Later decks should extend the same module with new sections or a second bank.

## Slide corrections and qualifications

### 1. Midget ganglion cells and the central pathway

**Slide issue:** one LGN/V1 slide says midget RGCs terminate in parvocellular layers “to form the magnocellular pathway.”

**Authoring decision:** use the established mapping:

- midget RGC → parvocellular stream;
- parasol RGC → magnocellular stream;
- small bistratified RGC → prominent koniocellular/S-cone-related stream.

No question preserves the erroneous midget-to-magnocellular wording.

### 2. ipRGC pigment

**Slide issue:** the extrastriate deck uses “melanin” in one place for the photopigment of intrinsically photosensitive retinal ganglion cells.

**Authoring decision:** use **melanopsin**. “Melanin” is retained only as a plausible distractor tagged as a misconception.

### 3. The classical duplex-retina label

The slides correctly introduce rods and cones as the traditional duplex retina while noting ipRGCs as a third intrinsically photosensitive class.

**Authoring decision:** questions say “classical rod-cone duplex system” where necessary and avoid implying that ipRGCs replace rods and cones in ordinary high-resolution image vision.

### 4. Foveal cone and S-cone wording

Statements such as “S cones are absent at the fovea” are too broad if interpreted as the whole anatomical fovea.

**Authoring decision:** use the qualified statement that S cones are sparse and largely absent from the **central foveola**, while occurring outside that central zone.

### 5. Foveal convergence ratios

The slide shorthand “nearly 1:1:1 cone:bipolar:RGC” is a useful teaching approximation but does not describe every central cone circuit. Detailed foveal circuitry includes separate ON and OFF midget pathways.

**Authoring decision:** assess **low convergence/private-line sampling**, not one universal numerical ratio.

### 6. Photoreceptor counts and numerical ratios

Values such as total rods, cones, ganglion cells, S-cone proportions and retinal convergence vary by measurement and source.

**Authoring decision:** focus on direction and functional consequence unless the stem explicitly says “according to the course deck.” This bank contains no high-stakes question whose correctness depends on one disputed population count.

### 7. Hermann grid explanation

The slide account using centre-surround antagonism and smaller foveal receptive fields is a useful classical teaching model but is not a complete modern account of every Hermann-grid variant.

**Authoring decision:** questions explicitly describe it as a **classical course-level account** and do not claim that lateral inhibition alone explains all grid phenomena.

### 8. Dark-adaptation timings

The slides use approximate milestones such as early rod contribution after one to two minutes, a rod-cone break near ten minutes and maximum sensitivity up to about forty minutes.

**Authoring decision:** assess the sequence and the meaning of the rod-cone break. Timings are described as approximate and are not treated as invariant values.

### 9. LGN orientation wording

Most classical LGN neurons are taught as concentric centre-surround and largely non-orientation-selective, although research identifies specialized exceptions, particularly in some koniocellular populations.

**Authoring decision:** use “most classical LGN cells” rather than “all LGN cells.”

### 10. Orientation and direction columns

One slide refers to neurons with the same orientation preference as “direction columns.”

**Authoring decision:** use **orientation columns** for shared edge-angle preference and reserve **direction selectivity** for preferred motion direction.

### 11. Hypercomplex terminology

“Hypercomplex cell” is historically important but “end-stopped cell” is the clearer functional term.

**Authoring decision:** use “end-stopped (historically hypercomplex)” in explanations.

### 12. V1 and disparity labels

The course uses crossed/near, uncrossed/far and zero disparity language.

**Authoring decision:** preserve this course alignment while avoiding the claim that one simple label captures all modern disparity-tuning classes.

### 13. Dorsal and ventral streams

The two-stream model is a useful organizing framework, but the pathways interact and are not completely independent.

**Authoring decision:** questions use:
- dorsal → parietal → spatial guidance/vision for action;
- ventral → temporal → object/form/vision for perception;
and explicitly avoid claiming complete separation.

### 14. Cortical blindness

The slide pattern of profound visual loss with preserved pupillary light reflexes is valid for bilateral occipital/striate damage after ocular and anterior pathway causes are excluded.

**Authoring decision:** clinical questions include bilateral occipital context, normal ocular findings and preserved pupil responses rather than treating any poor vision as cortical blindness.

### 15. Hemispatial neglect

Neglect is primarily an attentional-spatial disorder, not a simple hemianopic field defect.

**Authoring decision:** scenarios emphasize failure to attend to one side of space and avoid calling neglect an ocular field loss.

### 16. Blindsight

Blindsight does not establish extrasensory perception. It demonstrates that some visual information can guide behavior without normal conscious visual experience.

**Authoring decision:** the bank explicitly rejects paranormal or malingering-only explanations.

### 17. Prosopagnosia and visual agnosia

Prosopagnosia is treated as a face-recognition disorder associated with occipitotemporal/fusiform networks. Visual agnosia is divided conceptually into failures of percept formation or association, without forcing students to diagnose from insufficient information.

### 18. Irlen syndrome and broad “visual dyslexia” claims

These labels are controversial, heterogeneous or easily overstated. The uploaded extrastriate slides mention them, but the current evidence package is insufficient for strong causal assessment claims.

**Authoring decision:** no scored question treats Irlen syndrome as an established single neurological lesion or assumes that optometric treatment explains developmental dyslexia. These topics should be handled in a later evidence-specific lesson if retained.

### 19. Convergence insufficiency and attention

The slides list visual symptoms reported by patients with convergence insufficiency. Such symptoms are clinically relevant, but they should not be used to claim that convergence insufficiency is a proven cause of all reading or attention difficulties.

**Authoring decision:** no question makes that causal overreach.

### 20. Approximate “90% image-forming / 10% non-image-forming” split

This is a course-level simplification.

**Authoring decision:** the bank asks students to distinguish routes and functions, not to memorize the proportion as a universal anatomical constant.

## Assessment-quality decisions

### Majority familiar formats

The bank intentionally emphasizes single-best-answer and multiple-response questions. The nine supported formats are used only where they add educational value.

### Original questions

No commercial question-bank wording has been copied. Questions are newly authored from the supplied material and factual sources.

### Distractors

Distractors are designed around plausible misconceptions such as:

- sensation versus perception reversal;
- midget/parasol reversal;
- optic disc versus fovea;
- retinal versus cortical deficits;
- dorsal versus ventral reversal;
- melanopsin versus melanin;
- ipsilateral versus contralateral LGN layers;
- sensitivity versus acuity trade-off.

### Answer rationales

Every option-like item includes a rationale. Explanations teach the intended distinction rather than simply repeat the correct answer.

### Open responses

The two open-response items are excluded from the default automatically scored 50-question session. They remain useful for study practice and manual/self discussion.

### Images

All six proposed diagram assets are **original SVG briefs**, not copied slide screenshots:

1. retina landmarks/fundus;
2. retinal circuit;
3. photoreceptor distribution graph;
4. medial visual pathway/V1 location;
5. LGN layer organization;
6. dorsal and ventral streams.

Hotspot interaction labels are spatial and neutral before submission. Anatomical answer labels appear only in feedback.

## Implementation conclusion

The package is suitable for a **slide-aligned, internally verified study-practice experience**. It must not be described as a lecturer-approved examination bank. All items remain `draft` in the formal schema. Formal PR 8 review-campaign tooling remains available but is not required for this private owner/student workflow.
